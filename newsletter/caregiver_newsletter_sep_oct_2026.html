Important Medication Assistance Guidelines Updates
What a Home Care Aide may and may not do

Home Care Aides provide nonmedical services and may not administer medication. The guidance below explains what an aide may and may not do when assisting a client with self administered medication.

When in doubt, stop. If you feel uncertain whether alice.care permits a requested task, do not complete the task until you contact the alice.care office for guidance.
1. Accessing Preorganized Medication
✓ Yes, permitted
Question: A client keeps medications in a locked cabinet. May a Home Care Aide unlock and open the cabinet, remove a pill organizer that the client, a family member, or another authorized person prepared and separated, and make the organizer available to the client?

Answer: Yes. A Home Care Aide may unlock the cabinet, remove the prepared pill organizer, and open the organizer for the client, provided the aide does not handle the medication.

The aide assists with access to the medication container and does not handle or administer the medication. The client must take the medication independently.

2. Placing Medication Into Food
✕ Not permitted
Question: A client cannot swallow pills and states that they must take the pills with applesauce. If someone already prepared the medication in a pill organizer, may the Home Care Aide remove the pill from the appropriate compartment and place it into the applesauce for the client to eat independently?

Answer: No. A Home Care Aide may not remove medication from a pill organizer or place medication into food or a beverage.

Mixing medication into food or a beverage counts as medication administration, which falls outside the permitted scope of nonmedical home care services.

3. Feeding a Client Food Containing Medication
✕ Not permitted
Question: If an authorized person already placed or mixed medication into food, may a Home Care Aide feed that prepared food to a client who cannot feed themselves?

Answer: No. A Home Care Aide may not feed a client food or beverages containing medication.

Feeding medicated food or beverages introduces medication into the client's body and counts as medication administration, even when someone outside alice.care prepared the food.

If you discover medication mixed into a client's food or beverage, stop and contact the alice.care office for instructions.

4. Adding Thickener to Beverages
⏳ Pending guidance
Question: A client requires thickened liquids and cannot consume plain water or other thin liquids. May a Home Care Aide add a commercially available thickening powder to the client's beverages according to established instructions?

Answer: alice.care has requested additional clarification on this situation.

Although stores sell thickening powder over the counter as a food additive, a clinician may also prescribe it as a functional medical food. When a clinician prescribes it for a medical purpose, preparing or serving thickened beverages may require oversight from a licensed healthcare professional.

Until we share further guidance, contact the alice.care office before preparing or serving a beverage that requires a prescribed thickening product.

Accepting and Managing Service Assignments
Self accept, overtime approval, and commitment

We post open services on the job board, and caregivers receive an app notification when a new service becomes available. Caregivers with self accept access may review available services and assign themselves directly through the app.

Before accepting a service
  • Review the date, time, location, and service requirements.
  • Confirm that you can complete the entire service.
  • Check whether the service could place you into overtime.
  • Request approval before accepting a service that may result in overtime.
  • Request assignment approval when the Care Team must approve the assignment.

Once you accept a service, the client and Care Team rely on you to complete it. Accepting and then repeatedly declining services disrupts the client's care plan and makes consistent coverage difficult.

If a caregiver repeatedly accepts and then declines services, alice.care may change the caregiver's profile settings. This may include removing self accept access and requiring Care Team approval for future service assignments.

Accept only the services you feel prepared and authorized to complete. If an emergency prevents you from working an accepted service, notify the office immediately.

  • Service details lack clarity.
  • Caregivers feel unsure whether they qualify for an assignment.

  • Caregivers prefer extra confirmation before accepting a service.
Your feedback helps us improve the process. Call or text the office with your ideas, questions, or concerns about the job board and self assignment process, or write to tom@alice.care.

Proper Shift Documentation
Specific, factual, and objective notes

Accurate documentation forms an essential part of every service. Your notes create a record of the care you provided, help the Care Team monitor changes, support communication with the client's Responsible Party, and protect both the client and caregiver.

Complete your notes before ending the service whenever possible. Document the care you actually provided during the shift.

Include when applicable
  • The client's condition and mood when you arrived and throughout the shift
  • Personal care such as bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, brief changes, or mobility assistance
  • Assistance with transfers, walking, positioning, or mobility equipment
  • Meals or snacks you prepared
  • Approximately how much the client ate or drank
  • Household tasks such as laundry, dishes, or light housekeeping
  • Activities and cognitive or social engagement with the client
  • Medication reminders you provided
  • Changes in the client's condition, behavior, mobility, appetite, or routine
  • Falls, injuries, unusual events, or safety concerns
  • Issues you reported to the office or the client's family
  • The client's condition and location when you left

Write specific, factual, and objective notes about what occurred during the shift. Do not guess, diagnose, or include personal opinions.

Avoid vague statements like these
"Everything was good." "Client is fine." "Completed all tasks." "No changes."

These statements do not provide enough information about the care you delivered.

✓ Example of a complete shift note
"Client was awake and in good spirits upon arrival. Assisted with showering, dressing, and transferring from the wheelchair to the shower chair. Prepared scrambled eggs and toast. Client ate approximately 75% of breakfast and drank one glass of water. Completed one load of laundry and cleaned the kitchen. Client and caregiver worked on a puzzle and discussed family photos for cognitive and social engagement. Provided a medication reminder at 10:00 a.m. Client was resting comfortably in the living room when the shift ended. No falls or safety concerns were observed."

Traffic and Parking Responsibilities
What alice.care covers and what it does not

Caregivers must follow all traffic and parking laws while providing transportation or completing client related errands. This applies whether you drive your own vehicle or the client's vehicle.

alice.care does not pay or reimburse

  • Speeding tickets
  • Moving violations
  • Parking tickets
  • Towing charges caused by illegal parking
  • Other fines resulting from a caregiver's actions

Eligible paid parking

When an authorized service reasonably requires paid parking, alice.care covers the eligible parking expense you incur during the service. Keep the receipt or other proof of payment and follow the company reimbursement process.

Parking reimbursement excludes tickets, penalties, late fees, towing charges, and other violations.

Before parking, check all posted signs, time limits, permit requirements, and payment instructions. If a spot requires payment, complete it correctly and on time.

Mobile Phone Use During Services
Keep your attention on the client

Your attention must stay focused on the client throughout the service. alice.care does not permit personal mobile phone use while you provide care.

Allowed work related uses

  • Communicating with the alice.care office
  • Using the alice.care app
  • Completing required service documentation
  • Responding to an emergency
  • Completing an authorized task related to the client's care

Not allowed during a service

  • Personal calls or text messages
  • Social media
  • Entertainment
  • Shopping
  • Other unrelated activities

If you must handle an urgent personal matter, make sure the client stays safe and contact the office for guidance.

Report Unusual Events Immediately
Call the office first, then document

Notify the alice.care office immediately about any event that feels unexpected, significant, or related to the client's safety or well being. Examples include:

  • A fall or near fall
  • An injury
  • A sudden change in the client's condition or behavior
  • Property damage or a broken item of significance
  • A medication related concern
  • A transportation issue or accident
  • A safety hazard in the home
  • A conflict involving the client or another person
  • An emergency response or hospitalization
  • Any situation that may concern the client's Responsible Party

Do not wait until the end of the shift or rely only on a note in the app. Contact the office immediately so the Care Team can respond appropriately and communicate proactively with the client's Responsible Party.

The client's Responsible Party should never become the first person to inform alice.care about an incident during a service.

After you notify the office, document the event accurately and follow any additional instructions from the Care Team.

Protect Your Personal Contact Information
Keep communication in approved channels

Do not give clients or their family members your personal phone number or other private contact information. This policy protects caregivers from direct requests involving:

  • Schedule changes
  • Additional services
  • Personal needs or concerns
  • Requests outside the approved care plan
  • Communication outside appropriate work channels

The Care Team manages caregiver schedules. A Care Coach must review and confirm any request to change a service date, start time, end time, duration, or assignment.

Do not make private scheduling arrangements directly with a client or family member. If a client asks you to change the schedule, contact the office before agreeing to the request.
